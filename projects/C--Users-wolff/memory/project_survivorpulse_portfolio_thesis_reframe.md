On 2026-07-11 the founder challenged SurvivorPulse's foundational thesis directly: survivor pool entries don't behave like stock-portfolio holdings (continuous, roughly-symmetric returns). They behave like a chain of binary, barrier-style options — survive the week and keep a contingent claim on the pot, lose and the entry is instantly worth zero. This is a materially better theoretical lens than "manage it like an investment portfolio," and it reframes the objective function: not variance-reduction (Markowitz), but maximizing P(at least one entry survives to week W) — a union probability across correlated binary outcomes — with explicit modeling of prize-splitting when multiple owned entries co-survive.

**Why: this explains a finding that was previously just an empirical curiosity.** [[project_survivorpulse_unified_strategy_engine]]'s SST-759 finding (N entries modeled as N copies of one strategy collapses to the N=1 case, no diversification benefit) is now mechanistically explained rather than merely observed: 100%-correlated binary bets rise and fall together, so of course multiple identical entries buy you nothing. Diversification value only appears once entries are genuinely decorrelated. This also unifies with SST-775's field-size/differentiation research (correlation *with the field* is the same underlying mechanism as correlation *within your own portfolio*, just at a different level).
